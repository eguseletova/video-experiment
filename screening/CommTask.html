Introduction
Companies often use financial bonuses to motivate employees. But does the promise of a bonus actually improve the quality of work — or does it just make workers go faster? And does the answer change depending on whether workers know their performance is being monitored?

We predicted that financial incentives would improve both the speed and quality of work. We also predicted that adding performance monitoring — telling workers their output would be reviewed — would further boost quality, particularly when combined with a bonus.

Methods
We recruited 320 adults on an online platform to complete a data-coding task: reviewing short survey responses and assigning each one a category label. Participants were randomly assigned to one of four conditions in a 2×2 design:
  • Incentive: participants either received a flat payment regardless of performance (no bonus) or were told they would earn an additional $2 for every 10 responses coded correctly (bonus).
  • Monitoring: participants were either told nothing about review (no monitoring) or were told that a research assistant would spot-check 20% of their responses for accuracy (monitoring).
We measured two outcomes: the number of responses coded per minute (speed) and the percentage of responses coded correctly (accuracy).

Results
Offering a bonus significantly increased coding speed: participants in the bonus conditions coded an average of 4.2 responses per minute, compared to 3.1 in the no-bonus conditions. However, the bonus had no significant effect on accuracy overall.

The effect of monitoring depended on whether a bonus was also present. In the no-bonus conditions, monitoring had little effect on either speed or accuracy. But in the bonus conditions, adding monitoring significantly improved accuracy — from 71% correct (bonus only) to 84% correct (bonus + monitoring) — while leaving speed unchanged.
